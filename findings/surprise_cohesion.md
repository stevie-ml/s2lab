# Surprise Cohesion: Are a Poem's High-S₂ Tokens Semantically Unified?

**Date:** 2026-11-12
**Experiment:** `experiments/surprise_cohesion.py` → `results/surprise_cohesion.json`
**Corpus:** 398 of 411 poems with ≥3 clean high-S₂ tokens (S₂ ≥ 2.0, p_newline < 0.9)
**Builds on:** `semantic_field_of_surprise.md`, `s2_arc_typology.md`, `arc_shadow_synthesis.md`

---

## Research Question

When a poem has multiple high-S₂ moments — positions where the poet chose something GPT-2
found genuinely surprising — are those moments **semantically unified** (the surprise tokens
cluster together in embedding space) or **semantically scattered** (each spike is a local
effect with no global coherence)?

**Excess Cohesion** = (mean pairwise cosine similarity of surprise tokens) − (mean pairwise
cosine similarity of same-sized random sample from the poem's ordinary tokens).

A positive score means the poem's surprises share more semantic proximity than its
ordinary language. A negative score means surprise tokens are *more* semantically
dispersed than the poem's baseline.

---

## Global Finding: Poetry Defaults to Scattered Surprise

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Poems analyzed | 398 |
| Mean surprise cohesion | 0.2419 |
| Mean baseline cohesion | 0.2505 |
| **Mean excess cohesion** | **−0.0086** |
| Std excess cohesion | 0.0372 |
| % poems with positive excess cohesion | **41.5%** |

**The slight negative excess cohesion (−0.009) indicates that, on average, poetic surprise
tokens are semantically more scattered than a poem's ordinary language.** Poetry, as a whole,
uses surprise to diversify — not to consolidate — its semantic territory.

However, this global average conceals enormous variation across traditions.

## Key Interpretations

### 1. Haiku: Maximal Semantic Unity of Surprise

Haiku shows the highest excess cohesion (+0.036, 90% positive). The surprise tokens in a
haiku tend to cluster tightly — the juxtaposition of a few concrete images means the
poem's deviations from GPT-2's expectation all belong to the same semantic neighborhood.

> Example: **Chiyo-ni, "Morning Glory"** (excess cohesion: +0.210)
> Surprise tokens: `glory | buck | et | entangled`
> These tokens all orbit a single semantic core: the morning glory vine and the well bucket.

### 2. Beat Poetry: 100% Positive — Surprise as Thematic Force

Beat poetry achieves perfect cohesion of surprise (all 8 Beat poems positive). Beat poets
(Ginsberg, Kerouac, Ferlinghetti) tend to return obsessively to a core set of transgressive or
visionary images; their deviations from language norms are semantically concentrated.

### 3. Imagism: 0% Positive — Surprise as Juxtaposition

Every imagist poem in the corpus shows negative excess cohesion. This confirms the theoretical
program of Imagism: surprise is generated by juxtaposing semantically *unrelated* images
("In a Station of the Metro": faces + petals). The poem's high-S₂ tokens are maximally
dispersed across the semantic space precisely because that is the aesthetic goal.

### 4. OuLiPo and Language Poetry: Constraint Breaks Semantic Unity

OuLiPo (12% positive) and Language poetry (17% positive) produce the most scattered surprises
among major traditions, despite their very different projects. In OuLiPo, the formal constraint
forces surprising words regardless of semantic coherence — the constraint is structural, not
semantic. In Language poetry, the aesthetic goal is explicitly to disrupt semantic continuity,
so scattered surprise is intended.

### 5. Confessional vs. Contemporary: A Striking Divergence

Confessional poetry (Plath, Sexton, Lowell era: +0.012, 83% positive) uses surprise in a
unified way. Contemporary poetry (−0.029, only 21% positive) is among the most scattered.
This suggests that the therapeutic, autobiographical program of confessional poetry creates
semantic focus, while contemporary poetry has diversified into strategies that deliberately
avoid thematic unity.

are tightly clustered (all Romantic pastoral-erotic vocabulary), confirming that the poem
achieves its effect through concentrated, semantically unified surprise.

## Most Semantically Scattered Surprising Poems

| Excess Cohesion | Author | Title | Era |
|-----------------|--------|-------|-----|
| −0.097 | Joy Harjo | She Had Some Horses (opening) | contemporary |
| −0.112 | Arthur Symons | The Wanderer's Litany | triolet |
| −0.120 | Joy Harjo | She Had Some Horses | postcolonial |
| **−0.248** | Emmett Williams | For Spacious Skies | concrete |

Joy Harjo's "She Had Some Horses" appears at the bottom: `horses · bodies · sand · maps ·
drawn · ocean` — these are semantically diverse (topology, geography, anatomy, nature), which
matches the poem's formally catalogic, accumulative structure where surprise is generated
through unexpected COMBINATION rather than semantic focus.

Emmett Williams' concrete poem achieves maximum scatter: the visual arrangement of words
like `skies · spacious` creates surprise through syntactic/visual disruption, not semantic
clustering.

---

## Correlation: Cohesion vs. Overall Surprise Magnitude

**r(excess cohesion, avg S₂) = +0.099** — essentially zero.

A poem being generally more surprising does NOT predict whether its surprises are
semantically unified. The two dimensions (surprise magnitude vs. surprise coherence) are
nearly orthogonal — a poet can achieve high average S₂ with either concentrated or scattered
semantic deployment of that surprise.

## Theoretical Implications

This experiment suggests a **two-axis model** of poetic surprise strategy:

```
                   HIGH cohesion
                        │
           Haiku        │    Beat, Confessional
           Blake        │    Whitman
           ─────────────┼─────────────
           Low S2       │    High S2
           ─────────────┼─────────────
           Nursery      │    Surrealism
           rhyme        │    Language Poetry
           Ballad       │    Imagism
                        │
                   LOW cohesion
```

- **Quadrant 1** (high S₂, high cohesion): Concentrated, intense, semantically unified
  surprise. The conceit/thematic-obsession strategy.
- **Quadrant 2** (high S₂, low cohesion): Intense surprise through semantic juxtaposition.
  The montage/defamiliarization strategy.
- **Quadrant 3** (low S₂, low cohesion): Random-walking ordinary language. Minimal poetic
  signature.
- **Quadrant 4** (low S₂, high cohesion): Conventional language that stays within a single
  semantic domain. Technical prose, found poetry from specialized registers.

## Suggested Next Steps

1. **Within-tradition variation**: Are there imagist poems that DO show positive cohesion?
   What distinguishes them from the typical imagist pattern?

2. **Semantic field tagging of surprise clusters**: For poems with high excess cohesion,
   what semantic field do the surprise tokens belong to? Is it always the poem's "announced"
   theme, or can the high-S₂ tokens reveal a hidden subtheme?

3. **Cohesion × arc type**: Do ASCENT poems (S₂ builds toward the end) tend to have higher
   cohesion than DECLINE poems? The arc-shadow synthesis found 54.7% post-peak fulfillment;
   does cohesion predict whether the shadow word is fulfilled?

4. **Multi-poem author analysis**: For poets with multiple poems, is surprise cohesion a
   stable authorial fingerprint, or does it vary across individual works?
